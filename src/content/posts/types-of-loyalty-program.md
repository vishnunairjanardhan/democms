Customer loyalty is important for the success of any business. In today's competitive marketplace, retaining loyal customers is just as important as acquiring new ones. One of the most effective strategies for ensuring <a href="https://www.99minds.io/blog/repeat-customer" role="link" target="_blanck">repeat customers</a> is to implement loyalty programs. By understanding the various types of loyalty programs available, businesses can encourage repeat business, boost customer engagement, and ultimately drive revenue. According to research by <a href="https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hubfs/352767/Loyalty%20Report%202018%20Files/Bond_TheLoyaltyReport%202018%20US_Exec%20Summary.pdf" role="link" target="_blanck">Bond Brand Loyalty</a>, 77% of consumers are more likely to stick with businesses that offer loyalty programs. 

Over time, various loyalty strategies such as points schemes, tiered programs, and paid memberships have been used in retail. However, it's important to assess the success of these programs in fostering genuine loyalty before determining which type of loyalty program is ideal for your brand.

In this article, we'll explore the different types of loyalty programs, delve into the pros and cons of each with real-world examples, and provide you with a framework to identify the best loyalty program for your business.


<h2 id="What-is-a-Loyalty-Program?">What is a Loyalty Program?</h2>

Loyalty programs are strategic marketing initiatives designed to encourage repeat business and <a href="https://www.99minds.io/blog/build-customer-loyalty" role="link" target="_blanck">build customer loyalty</a>. These programs offer various rewards, such as discounts, exclusive offers, and points-based systems, to encourage customer loyalty. By implementing different types of loyalty programs, businesses can cater to diverse customer preferences and enhance the overall customer experience. This can help in retaining customers, increasing customer satisfaction, and fostering long-term relationships, ultimately contributing to the success and growth of the business.

<h2 id="Different-Types-of-Loyalty-Programs">Different Types of Loyalty Programs</h2>

Understanding the different types of loyalty programs is crucial for designing one that best suits your business needs. Here are eight popular types:
<ol>
<li>
<h3>Types of Loyalty Programs: Points-Based Programs</h3>

One of the most popular types of loyalty programs is point-based. In this system, customers earn points for each purchase they make, which they can later redeem for rewards such as discounts, free products, or exclusive offers. This system encourages repeat purchases by giving customers a tangible reason to come back.

Example: <a href="https://www.starbucks.com/rewards" role="link" target="_blanck">Starbucks</a> Rewards program The Starbucks Rewards program offers customers multiple ways to earn stars, which can be redeemed for various rewards. Customers earn 1 star per dollar when they pay with cash or a credit/debit card at the register. By scanning their Starbucks app before paying, they ensure that the purchase is linked to their Rewards account. To earn 2 stars per dollar, customers can preload funds onto their digital Starbucks Card using the app or register a physical gift card, converting it into a digital card. This not only speeds up the checkout process but also consolidates balances from multiple cards, maximizing the star-earning potential.

Additionally, customers can save their credit/debit card or PayPal information in the app, simplifying the checkout process further. This feature allows them to order ahead or scan and pay at the register in one seamless step, ensuring that they earn stars with every purchase. The Starbucks Rewards program, therefore, provides flexibility in earning stars and convenient payment methods, enhancing the overall customer experience.
</li>

<div class="m-auto w-full lg:px-34 flex justify-center">
    <img loading="lazy" role="img" src="/assets/blog/Screenshot 2024-07-15 163913.webp" alt="Starbucks Rewards program">
</div>

<b>Pros:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Simple and Easy to Understand:</b> Customers can easily understand the concept of earning points and redeeming them for rewards, leading to widespread adoption and engagement.</li>
<li><b>Encourages Repeat Purchases:</b> By collecting points, customers are motivated to make more purchases to achieve their reward goals, resulting in increased sales and customer retention.</li>
<li><b>Flexible Rewards Options:</b> Businesses can offer a variety of rewards to appeal to different customer preferences, catering to a diverse customer base.</li>
<li><b>Low Barrier to Entry:</b> The program is free to join, which makes it easy for new customers to sign up without any initial costs.</li>
<li><b>Gateway to Greater Loyalty:</b> Acts as an entry point into a larger loyalty ecosystem, using engagement tactics to transform occasional customers into regular ones.</li>
</ul>

<b>Cons</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Can Be Perceived as Transactional:</b> Customers might view the relationship as purely transactional rather than developing a deeper loyalty to the brand. This can limit emotional attachment.</li>
<li><b>Administrative Management:</b> Managing points accrual and redemption can require significant administrative resources and technology, which can be costly and complex, especially for small businesses.</li>
<li><b>Delayed Gratification:</b> Reward systems that require customers to accumulate points over time may not satisfy the desire for instant gratification that many modern consumers expect.</li>
</ul>

<li>
<h3>Types of Loyalty Programs: Tiered Programs</h3>

Tiered programs offer varying levels of rewards based on a customer's spending or engagement. This hierarchy of rewards, a common model in different types of loyalty programs, encourages customers to increase their spending to unlock higher tiers, each providing progressively better rewards. This structure creates a sense of achievement and exclusivity, encouraging customers to increase their spending to reach higher tiers.

Each tier comes with improved benefits, adding a gamification element to the loyalty program. Gamification incorporates addictive elements from video games into the program, making it more engaging and enjoyable for customers. Features like progress bars and milestones keep customers returning to earn points and unlock new rewards. This approach makes the loyalty program more enjoyable and motivating, driving continuous engagement and higher spending.

Example: <a href="https://www.sephora.com/beauty/profile-beautyinsider" role="link" target="_blanck">Sephora's Beauty</a> Insider program is a tiered loyalty program designed to reward customers for their purchases and encourage higher spending through progressively more attractive benefits. This type of loyalty program has three levels: Insider, VIB (Very Important Beauty Insider), and Rouge. Each tier offers a range of perks and rewards that increase in value as members move up the tiers. 

The Insider Tier is free to join and offers benefits such as free standard shipping with no minimum purchase required, the ability to apply 500 points for $10 off a qualifying purchase, and 10% off during seasonal savings events. 

Members who spend $350 annually enter the VIB Tier, which includes all Insider benefits plus 15% off during seasonal savings events, 3X points during point multiplier events, and an end-of-year discount. 
Those who spend $1000 annually are elevated to the Rouge Tier, enjoying all VIB benefits, 20% off during seasonal savings events, 4X points during point multiplier events, and exclusive perks such as a private hotline, access to exclusive events, and the option to exchange 2,500 points for $100 off a purchase.
</li>

<div class="m-auto w-full lg:px-34 flex justify-center">
    <img loading="lazy" role="img" src="/assets/blog/Sephora-Beauty.webp" alt="Sephora's Beauty Insider program">
</div>

<b>Pros:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Encourages Increased Spending:</b> Customers are motivated to spend more to reach higher tiers and unlock better rewards. This can lead to higher average order values and increased sales.</li>
<li><b>Creates Exclusivity and Status:</b> Higher tiers offer exclusive benefits, making customers feel valued and special. This sense of exclusivity can build customer loyalty and brand affinity.</li>
<li><b>Focuses on Higher-Value Customers:</b> By prioritizing customers who spend more or engage frequently, tiered loyalty programs can allocate resources and rewards more effectively, ensuring that top-tier customers feel valued and incentivized to maintain their loyalty.</li> 
<li><b>Enhanced Experiences Across Tiers:</b> Unlike traditional points-based programs, tiered systems can offer differentiated experiences that escalate with customer loyalty levels. This allows for more meaningful rewards that align with the amount a customer interacts or spends with your brand.</li>
<li><b>Greater Customization and Differentiation:</b>Tiered programs provide more opportunities for customization, allowing businesses to tailor rewards and experiences to specific customer segments. This customization helps in distinguishing a brand from competitors by offering unique benefits that are tailored to the customer’s preferences and spending habits.</li> 
</ul>

<b>Cons:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Complex to Manage:</b> Communicating the benefits and managing the different tiers can be complicated. Ensuring that customers understand and are motivated by the tiered system requires clear communication and marketing efforts.</li>
<li><b>Risk of Alienation:</b> Customers who remain in lower tiers might feel neglected or undervalued. This can lead to dissatisfaction and potentially drive them to competitors.</li>
</ul>

<li>
<h3>Types of Loyalty Programs: Paid Membership Programs</h3>

Paid membership programs, also known as premium loyalty programs, require customers to pay a fee to access exclusive benefits and rewards. These types of loyalty programs are designed to provide significant value that justifies the cost, such as free shipping, special discounts, early access to sales, and premium content. The immediate perks of premium loyalty create a sense of instant gratification, which is increasingly important in today’s market.

According to a <a href="https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/coping-with-the-big-switch-how-paid-loyalty-programs-can-help-bring-consumers-back-to-your-brand" role="link" target="_blanck">McKinsey</a> report on loyalty programs, members of paid membership programs are 60% more likely to increase their spending with your brand, compared to just a 30% increase for free loyalty programs. This demonstrates the greater impact of paid membership programs in fostering customer loyalty and driving higher spending.

Example: <a href="https://www.amazon.com/amazonprime" role="link" target="_blanck">Amazon Prime</a> offers a comprehensive membership program that extends beyond traditional shopping benefits. For an annual fee of $119, members are assured they receive the best possible shopping advantages, no matter when they shop. These include free two-day shipping on millions of items, exclusive discounts, and special offers. However, the value of Amazon Prime isn't confined to these transactional benefits.

The membership also includes experiential perks such as access to Prime Video, allowing members to stream a vast library of movies, TV shows, and original content. This entertainment option adds significant value, enriching members' daily lives beyond shopping.

Overall, Amazon Prime delivers extensive value, integrating both shopping convenience and entertainment into a single membership. This combination appeals to a broad audience, fostering customer loyalty and increasing overall spending. Members find that the benefits of the program touch various aspects of their lives, making the membership fee well worth it.
</li>

<div class="m-auto w-full lg:px-34 flex justify-center">
    <img loading="lazy" role="img" src="/assets/blog/amazon-prime.webp" alt="Amazon Prime paid membership program">
</div>

<b>Pros:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Generates Immediate Revenue:</b> Membership fees provide an upfront revenue stream. This can be particularly beneficial for cash flow and funding ongoing business operations.</li>
<li><b>Attracts Highly Engaged Customers:</b> Customers who pay for membership are likely to be more loyal and engaged. The upfront investment indicates a higher level of commitment to the brand.</li>
</ul>

<b>Cons:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Requires Compelling Value Proposition:</b> To attract and retain members, the benefits must clearly outweigh the cost. Businesses need to continuously deliver high-value perks to justify the membership fee.</li>
<li><b>Potentially Limits Customer Base:</b> Some customers may be unwilling to pay for membership, limiting the program's reach. This can result in a smaller, but more engaged, customer base.</li>
</ul>

<li>
<h3>Types of Loyalty Programs: Cashback Programs</h3>

Cashback programs reward customers with a percentage of their purchase amount as cash back. This can be in the form of direct cash, store credit, or discounts on future purchases. These types of loyalty programs provide immediate, tangible rewards, which can be very appealing to customers.

Example: <a href="https://www.rakuten.com/" role="link" target="_blanck">Rakuten</a> offers a cashback program where customers earn a percentage of their purchase amount back when shopping through their platform. This straightforward approach has made it a popular choice among consumers looking for direct financial rewards. The cashback percentage varies depending on the retailer and can range from 1% to over 10%, making it attractive for a wide range of purchases. For instance, if a retailer offers 5% cashback and a customer spends $100, they will receive $5 back.

Rakuten partners with thousands of retailers across various categories, including fashion, electronics, travel, and more. To participate, customers need to create a free account on Rakuten's website or app, find their desired retailer through the Rakuten platform, and make a purchase. The cashback is then credited to their Rakuten account and can be withdrawn via PayPal or check.

In addition to regular cashback offers, Rakuten occasionally provides special promotions and increased cashback rates during events like Black Friday or Cyber Monday. New users also benefit from an extra 10% cashback on purchases made in their first 7 days, enhancing the appeal of joining the program.

Rakuten's extensive network of partners and the ease of earning cashback has resulted in over 20 million members and $3.2 billion paid to members since 1999. With an average cashback per member of $90.16 in 2023 and more than 29,000 5-star reviews on Trustpilot, Rakuten continues to be a trusted and valuable service for savvy shoppers.
</li>

<div class="m-auto w-full lg:px-34 flex justify-center">
    <img loading="lazy" role="img" src="/assets/blog/rakuten.webp" alt="Rakuten cashback program">
</div>

<b>Pros:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Direct and Tangible Rewards:</b> Customers appreciate the immediate financial benefits of cashback. This transparency makes the rewards highly attractive and easy to understand.</li>
<li><b>Encourages Frequent Purchases:</b> Regular cashback incentives encourage customers to make more frequent purchases. This can drive <a href="https://www.99minds.io/blog/repeat-customer" role="link" target="_blanck">repeat customers</a> and customer loyalty.</li>
</ul>

<b>Cons:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Can Be Costly:</b> Providing cashback can be expensive for businesses, especially if not managed carefully. The financial outlay for cashback rewards needs to be balanced with the benefits of increased sales.</li>
<li><b>May Not Foster Emotional Loyalty:</b> Cashback programs are often viewed as financial transactions rather than relationship-building efforts. This can limit the development of deeper emotional connections with the brand.</li>
</ul>

<li>
<h3>Types of Loyalty Programs: Value-Based Programs</h3>

Value-based programs are designed to resonate with customers by aligning with their personal values and beliefs. These types of loyalty programs offer rewards that reflect shared principles, such as making charitable donations or supporting eco-friendly initiatives. By incorporating these elements, businesses can build deeper emotional connections with their customers. When customers see that a company supports causes they care about, it fosters a sense of loyalty and trust. This emotional bond goes beyond traditional transactional relationships, creating a community of customers who feel personally invested in the company’s mission and values.

Example: <a href="https://www.toms.com/" role="link" target="_blanck">TOMS</a> One for One program distributes one pair of shoes to a needy child for every pair purchased. This value-based strategy appeals to socially concerned consumers, strengthening their emotional connection to the company. TOMS has created a strong brand identity based on its dedication to social good, attracting people who value ethical consumption.
</li>

<div class="m-auto w-full lg:px-34 flex justify-center">
    <img loading="lazy" role="img" src="/assets/blog/toms.webp" alt="TOMS One for One program">
</div>

<b>Pros:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Builds Emotional Connections:</b> Aligning with customers' values fosters deeper, more meaningful loyalty. Customers feel good about supporting a brand that shares their values.</li>
<li><b>Differentiates the Brand:</b> Promoting shared values can distinguish a brand in a crowded market. This differentiation can attract like-minded customers and enhance brand reputation.</li>
</ul>

<b>Cons:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>May Not Appeal to All Customers:</b> Not every customer may prioritize the same values, potentially limiting the program's appeal. Businesses need to carefully select causes that resonate with a significant portion of their customer base.</li>
<li><b>Challenging to Measure Impact:</b> The impact of value-based rewards can be harder to quantify compared to financial rewards. Measuring the ROI of such programs requires tracking customer sentiment and engagement.</li>
</ul>

<li>
<h3>Types of Loyalty Programs: Coalition Programs</h3>

Coalition programs, categorized under the umbrella of types of loyalty programs, involve multiple businesses partnering to offer a shared loyalty program. Customers can earn and redeem rewards across different brands, increasing the value and flexibility of the program.

Example: <a href="https://www.airmiles.ca/" role="link" target="_blanck">Air Miles</a> is a coalition program where customers earn miles on purchases at various partner retailers. These miles can be redeemed for travel, merchandise, and experiences, offering significant value to participants. Air Miles' extensive network of partners enhances its appeal, providing customers with numerous opportunities to earn and redeem rewards.
</li> 

<div class="m-auto w-full lg:px-34 flex justify-center">
    <img loading="lazy" role="img" src="/assets/blog/air-miles.webp" alt="Air Miles coalition program">
</div>

<b>Pros:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Increases Earning and Redemption Opportunities:</b> Customers can earn and redeem points at multiple brands, enhancing the program's value. This broadens the appeal and utility of the loyalty program.</li>
<li><b>Enhances Brand Exposure:</b> Partnering with other businesses can increase brand visibility and attract new customers. This mutual benefit helps all participating brands grow their customer base.</li>
<li><b>Many of the Operational Costs are Shared:</b> By partnering with multiple brands, the operational costs of running the loyalty program, including marketing and rewards funding, are shared among the partners. This collaboration not only reduces expenses but also increases earning and redemption opportunities for customers across various brands, enhancing the program's overall value.</li>
</ul>

<b>Cons:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Complex to Manage and Coordinate:</b> Collaboration between multiple businesses can be challenging. Coordinating rewards points accrual, and redemption processes requires effective communication and management.</li>
<li><b>Requires Strong Partnerships:</b> Success depends on the strength and alignment of the partner businesses. Any issues with one partner can affect the entire coalition program.</li>
<li><b>Coalition-Owned Data:</b> The data collected is usually owned by the coalition and siloed away from the individual retailers</li>
</ul>

<li>
<h3>Types of Loyalty Programs: Game-Based Programs</h3>

Game-based programs use gamification elements to engage customers. These types of loyalty programs often involve challenges, levels, and rewards for participation and achievements, making the loyalty experience fun and interactive.

Example: <a href="https://www.nike.com/" role="link" target="_blanck">NikePlus</a> incorporates gamification through its app, rewarding users with points for completing workouts and participating in challenges. These points can be redeemed for exclusive products and experiences, fostering engagement and loyalty. NikePlus leverages fitness challenges and social features to keep users motivated and engaged.
</li>

<div class="m-auto w-full lg:px-34 flex justify-center">
    <img loading="lazy" role="img" src="/assets/blog/nikeplus.webp" alt="NikePlus Game-Based Programs">
</div>

<b>Pros:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Highly Engaging:</b> Gamification elements can make the loyalty program more enjoyable and engaging for customers. This increased engagement can lead to higher customer retention and loyalty.</li>
<li><b>Encourages Frequent Interaction:</b> Regular challenges and rewards motivate customers to interact frequently with the brand. This continuous interaction keeps the brand top-of-mind.</li>
</ul>

<b>Cons:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Requires Investment in Design and Technology:</b> Developing and maintaining a gamified loyalty program can be costly and complex. Businesses need to invest in technology and design to create an effective program.</li>
<li><b>Can Be Complex to Maintain:</b> Keeping the gamification elements fresh and exciting requires continuous effort and innovation. Stale or repetitive challenges can lead to customer disengagement.</li>
</ul>

<li>
<h3>Types of Loyalty Programs: Subscription-Based Programs</h3>

Subscription-based programs offer exclusive benefits and content to subscribers, typically in exchange for a recurring fee. These types of loyalty programs provide ongoing value and foster a sense of community among subscribers.

Example: <a href="https://www.netflix.com/" role="link" target="_blanck">Netflix</a> offers a subscription service where members receive unlimited access to a vast library of movies, TV shows, and original content. This model has been highly successful in retaining subscribers and generating steady revenue. Netflix's continuous addition of new content ensures ongoing value for its subscribers.
</li>

<div class="m-auto w-full lg:px-34 flex justify-center">
    <img loading="lazy" role="img" src="/assets/blog/netflix.webp" alt="Netflix Subscription-Based Programs">
</div>

<b>Pros:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Provides Steady Revenue:</b> Recurring subscription fees ensure a stable revenue stream. This predictability helps businesses plan and invest in long-term strategies.</li>
<li><b>Attracts Highly Engaged Customers:</b> Subscribers are often more committed and engaged with the brand. The recurring nature of subscriptions indicates a deeper level of customer loyalty.</li>
</ul>

<b>Cons:</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Requires Continuous Delivery of Value:</b> To retain subscribers, businesses must consistently offer valuable content and benefits. Failing to do so can lead to high churn rates.</li>
<li><b>Potentially Limits Customer Base:</b> Subscription fees may deter some potential customers from joining. This can result in a smaller, but more dedicated, customer base.</li>
</ul>
</ol>
