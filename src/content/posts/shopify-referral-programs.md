<h2 id="How-Shopify-Referral-Programs-Works">How a Shopify Referral Program Works</h2>

Shopify referral programs helps you reward customers for bringing in new business, usually through discounts, store credit, or free products. This encourages existing customers to spread the word about your brand while giving their friends and family an incentive to make a purchase.

When a customer joins your <a href="https://www.99minds.io/blog/referral-program-for-small-businesses">referral program</a>, they receive a unique referral link. When they share it, their referred friends will see a discount code or other reward at checkout, depending on how you set up your program. This simple process boosts word-of-mouth marketing and encourages repeat purchases.

Since Shopify doesn’t offer a built-in referral program, you’ll need to integrate a third-party provider like 99minds. These apps seamlessly connect with your store, making it easy to launch and manage a referral program that drives growth and customer retention.

<h2 id="Different-types-of-Shopify-Referral-Programs">Different Types of Shopify Referral Programs</h2>

When setting up Shopify referral programs, you’ll typically choose between two main types: one-sided and two-sided programs. Each has its benefits depending on your business goals.
<ul>
<li>Two-Sided Referral Programs: Both the referrer and the referred customer receive a reward once an eligible purchase is made. This approach is ideal for acquiring new customers while keeping existing ones engaged.</li>
<li>One-Sided Referral Programs: Only the referrer gets rewarded, allowing businesses to offer more significant incentives to existing customers without affecting acquisition costs. This strategy works well for improving customer retention.</li>

<img src="/assets/blog/friends-referral.jpg" class="mx-auto block" alt="types-of-referral-programs">

</ul>
Apart from the reward structure, how customers interact with Shopify referral programs also matters. There are two common formats: widget-based referral programs and integrated loyalty pages.

<h3>Widget-Based Referral Programs</h3>

A widget-based referral program appears as a floating widget on your Shopify store, allowing customers to interact with the referral program from anywhere on the site.

<h4>Pros of Widget-Based Referral Programs</h4>
<ul>
<li>Takes up minimal space on your website—no need for a dedicated page.</li>
<li>Keeps the referral program visible, acting as a constant reminder.</li>
<li>Easy for customers to access and share their referral links quickly.</li>
</ul>

<h4>Cons of Widget-Based Referral Programs</h4>
<ul>
<li>Customers may need to visit a help page for additional details.</li>
<li>A constant reminder may feel intrusive to some users.</li>
</ul>

Despite these potential drawbacks, widget-based referral programs can be highly effective. For instance, Galen Leather uses this approach and consistently attracts over 300 new referral sign-ups per month, generating impressive sales growth.

<h3>Referral Programs Within Integrated Loyalty Pages</h3>
Alternatively, some businesses embed their <a href="https://www.99minds.io/blog/referral-marketing-strategy">referral programs</a> into a dedicated loyalty page within their Shopify store. These pages are usually placed in the "Your Account" section, allowing customers to find and engage with the program at their convenience.

<h4>Pros of Integrated Loyalty Page Referral Programs</h4>
<ul>
<li>Can contribute to SEO efforts by driving organic traffic.</li>
<li>A constant reminder may feel intrusive to some users.</li>
<li>Customers can access referral details when they need them without a floating widget.</li>
</ul>

<h4>Cons of Integrated Loyalty Page Referral Programs</h4>
<ul>
<li>Without frequent visibility, customers may forget about the referral program.</li>
<li>Too much information on a single page may overwhelm users.</li>
</ul>

Brands like REN Clean Skincare have successfully embedded their referral programs within their loyalty pages. Their loyalty members contribute 38% of total revenue, and the program has driven a 68% increase in customer spending—proving that this approach can be incredibly effective.

<h2 id="Why-Referral-Programs-Work">Why Referral Programs Work</h2>
<ol role="list">
<li><h3>Customers Trust Referrals More Than Ads</h3></li>

Paid ads and SEO can help drive traffic, but research from Sprout Social reveals that a friend’s recommendation is the most influential factor in getting U.S. customers to purchase from social channels. Additionally, Gartner found that word-of-mouth recommendations drive 31% of purchases in the electronics sector.

Since referrals come from trusted sources, they are often a more cost-effective way to acquire new customers compared to traditional advertising.

<li><h3>Referral & Loyalty Programs Improve Customer Retention</h3></li>

A landmark study from Schmitt, Skiera, and Van den Bulte in the Journal of Marketing found that referred customers tend to have a higher contribution margin and long-term value compared to non-referred ones.

Since many loyalty programs include built-in referral incentives, businesses that combine the two strategies can increase retention while attracting new customers.

<li><h3>Referred Customers Spend More</h3></li>

Research from the American Marketing Association expands on these findings, showing that referred customers have a 16–25% higher customer lifetime value (CLV) than non-referred customers.

At 99minds data consistently shows that businesses implementing referral programs see higher average order values (AOV). A notable example is <a href="https://www.99minds.io/case-study/calico">Shopcalico</a>, which successfully tripled its AOV after launching a referral-integrated loyalty program.
